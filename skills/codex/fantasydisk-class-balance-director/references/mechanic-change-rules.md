# Mechanic Change Rules

Use mechanic changes when a weapon or class cannot hit the balance target without losing its identity.

## Solo Weak

Consider:

- Focused repeat hit on the same durable target.
- Mark, wound, expose, or ramping damage that rewards sustained contact.
- Execute or bonus damage below a health threshold.
- Reduced setup time against elites and bosses.
- Single-target shard, echo, or aftershock that does not also explode AoE power.

Avoid:

- Flat damage buffs that also overbuff AoE.
- Turning a crowd weapon into a generic single-target weapon.

## AoE or Crowd Weak

Consider:

- Cleave, splash, chain, pierce, fan, arc, lingering zone, or orbit.
- Better target distribution when multiple enemies are nearby.
- Target cap changes with falloff.
- Delayed pulse or hazard zone for area denial.
- Summon retargeting or multi-target attack cadence.

Avoid:

- Infinite overlap that scales without a cap.
- Large area plus full single-target damage with no tradeoff.

## Defense Weak

Consider:

- Brief shield, absorb, dodge, guard, parry, displacement, knockback, stagger, slow, or control window.
- Sustain within existing regen and vampirism caps.
- Safer range only if the weapon actually supports kiting.
- Summon body-blocking, taunt, or intercept only if implemented and testable.

Avoid:

- Uncapped healing loops.
- Permanent immunity or control chains.
- Defensive mechanics that exist only in description text.

## Too Strong

Consider:

- Target cap, falloff, shorter duration, longer setup, reduced overlap, cooldown, reload, commitment, smaller safe range, or lower defensive uptime.
- Split strength by axis: strong solo but weaker AoE, strong AoE but weaker boss pressure, strong defense but lower damage.

Avoid:

- Removing the weapon's signature identity.
- Flattening all weapons into the same DPS shape.

## Identity Preservation

Every class should have three weapons with different play patterns. Different means at least two of these vary:

- Range or positioning risk.
- Target geometry.
- Burst versus sustained cadence.
- Setup versus instant payoff.
- Single-target versus crowd focus.
- Defensive contribution.
- Stat scaling or buildcraft hook.
- Summon, zone, projectile, melee, beam, aura, or control archetype.

If two weapons differ only by icon, color, or small number changes, treat that as a design failure.
